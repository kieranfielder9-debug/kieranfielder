Tracklane · a design studyProduct · Customers · Docs · Privacy
HeadlineSeven words. It names the job (ship software) and the pain it removes (busywork). The grey gradient makes the large type feel softer.
HierarchyThe headline is about 4× the body size. The white button is the only bright solid shape, so your eye lands on it second.
VisualThe real product UI, cropped and faded at the bottom. It shows the product instead of describing it, and the fade pulls you down the page.
RestraintOne accent colour, used only for a soft glow and a status dot. On a dark page, colour used sparingly makes a strong impression.

Tidepool · a design studyFeatures · Pricing · Help · Privacy
LayoutCentred stack becomes a split layout. The audience wants to see the product working at a glance, so the visual moves up beside the words.
VisualA lane board with real pool details: lanes, swimmer levels, class sizes, water temperature. It shows the job being done, as Tracklane's screenshot did.
Call to action"Start building" becomes "Book a 15-minute demo". Busy school owners respond to a short, low-risk commitment.
TrustA specific number ("420 schools, 1.2M lessons") sits right under the buttons, where a cautious buyer hesitates.
What changed and why

Same bones, different audience

Each decision traces back to who is visiting and what they need to feel before they click.

ElementTracklane (developers)Tidepool (swim schools)
MoodCalm, precise, fastBright, friendly, reassuring
ColourNear-black with one muted indigoPale water with deep teal, plus a sunny yellow for highlights
TypeNeutral sans, tight letter spacing, medium weightRounded, chunky display face at extra-bold weight
LayoutStacked, left-aligned, wide screenshot belowSplit: words left, product right
Main button"Start building" (self-serve sign-up)"Book a 15-minute demo" (talk to a human)
ProofCustomer logo strip under the visualSpecific usage numbers right under the buttons
ShapesSharp 8px corners, thin bordersPill buttons, soft 20px cards, a wave divider
